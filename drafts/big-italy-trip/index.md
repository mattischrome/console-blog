In September we went to Italy for a week. We flew into Palermo, then took a coach ride across Sicily to Syracuse. After a trip up Mount Etna, we took the Intercity Notte train overnight to Milan complete with ferry ride for both us and the train. We followed this with four nights in Milan, complete with a trip to Leonardo da Vinci experience.

Back in January 2017 we went to Italy for a weekend, which I wrote about wrote about on the blog as [a little trip to Italy](/posts/a-little-trip-to-italy/). This, by comparison, was a big trip to Italy.

## Palermo

We flew out to Palermo, which is one of the more interesting plane landings I've experienced. Sicily is an interestingly shaped island as you come in on its westernmost point over some beautiful rock formations. Less interesting was the really long queue at immigration. We took the train from Palermo Airport, which took about 50 minutes into the central and found our accommodation. It was a nice high-cielinged appartment quite close to the station, accessible by the kind of lift you see in movies.

We walked into centre of Palermo and tried to get ourselves oriented. We had had some lunch and then went off to find a rather majestic looking fountain. Although in the heat, the fountains were trickling at a rather low pace. I think it was about 28C the day we landed. We soon took refuge again from said heat and had a cup of tea and a cake.

We trekked around Palermo some more and encountered a rather graffiti-strewn area. As Ingrid posed for her picture, nearby teenagers were letting off firecrackers in the attempt to intimidate us, but we just laughed. This was very much the non-touristy side of Palermo as discovered by people who had been up since two thirty that morning. We figured out where we wanted to eat that evening and promptly headed back to the apartment for a nana nap.

That evening was a gluten-free pizza place. Not being a very good nana-napper, I was so tired I could barely finish my pizza. I definitely didn't have room for dessert, which almost never happens. Next day we went out for a walk to the bus station so we knew where our bus to Syracuse was going from. Then we went to through the station and past the ferry port and had a look at more of Palermo.

I was completely disoriented and had no idea where we were going but eventually we found the botanic gardens. There were very nice Banyan trees. They are wonderful trees that are a sort of colony tree: they send down multiple shoots and so one tree can look like a whole forest of independent trees, but they're actually all one tree. We saw the Italian tree of the year 2022 among others. I could have stayed in that botanic garden probably the whole day (even the bins were photogenic) but Brian and Ingrid were keen to get on.

We walked down to the sea front and had a look at the coast. We saw some cargo ships off in the distance and the harbour. We walked along and it was quite obviously very much on the shoulder season, what is probably a hive of activity in the summer was quite quiet place. We found a nice seafood restaurant that seemed to be frequented by locals. Although I'm not a big fan of seafood, so I had pasta a la norma: a Sicilian delicacy of tomato sauce and aubergines. It was very nice.

We walked back along the coast and then back into Palermo, with the intention of heading back to the accommodation a different way to previously. This let us see a hidden side of the city, walking through drought-parched parks one alleyway over from a tourist drag of restaurants. More random fireworks. On the way back to the accommodation we saw another collection of local shops, this time lots of linens and clothes and IT. After a nap, we went out for dinner at a coeliac-friendly restaurant with everything gluten-free being prepared in a separate kitchen. Walking home we stopped into a sex shop, it was quite eye-opening, though most devices for sale in there were more preoccupied with opening up more than just your eyes. We also dipped into Lidl for croissants to eat on the bus the next morning. 

## To Syracuse

On Sunday morning we left Palermo behind, taking a coach across Sicily to Syracuse. It takes about three and a half hours. For 15€ it's great value. We snaked out of Palermo, seemingly stuck behind other suburban busses. But once you get out of the city, the views are incredible. The undulating countryside reminded me of travelling in South America. About an hour in we were riding on a bridge across the landscape, an entire road on stanchions essentially like you're on one long bridge. At the moment both lanes are one road, but part way through the journey we saw that they are currently building another road lower down, presumably for two lanes in each direction. The new sections were progressing towards each other in the middle from the outside in. So you've got a sort of rundown from above of the bridge roads that were being built.

We had a ten-minute pee break in the middle. I'm glad I took advantage of this. We pulled up at a random service station and the driver said to everyone "right we meet back here in ten minutes" and gestured the start of the timer on his phone. We all wandered back after ten minutes. The driver did a headcount and after the international sign language for everybody's here, we were on our way again. 

After the pit stop it was time for tunnels, these were really cool, although some were recently built and didn't have any lights in. Once we were through those, my mind started to play tricks on me. Looking up at the steep-sided hills, it looked at one moment there was a town up there with houses carved into the rock, the next it was just rocks. Some sort of bizarre illusion. Or maybe I was micro-napping!

Suddenly we had arrived in Syracuse and went looking for our accommodation. The bus station and the area around it seemed pretty ordinary, a truth of all bus stations everywhere I guess. It was a humid day and grey clouds glowered down on us as we trundled through the streets with our suitcases. Lots of dog poo to dodge and lots of cobbles that we couldn't. After checking out the airbnb, we went for a walk. One of the first things we found was a rainbow, one that persisted as we headed for the beach. We took lots of photos of that and went down to the sea. We found a restaurant that we wanted to have dinner at and then wandered on. We found the road over to Ortigia, the little island attached to Syracuse that we were planning on visiting the next day. Then walked back and we got into the restaurant, sensing that there was rain imminent, we sat indoors.  Ingrid and I had lovely Brazilian beef steaks. We had a very nice bottle of wine between us which might have been more expensive than the one we ordered because I think the waiter did something wrong with the wine - he was pretty green. We staggered home and slept well.

## Ortigia

We went to Ortigia the next day. We started out the temple of Apollo and then walked through the thronging markets - the first part was what I call the "Chinese Cargo Crate dump" with all the usual tat piled high while the stall owners work out whether to try and get your attention or to shrink back in embarassment. More fun was when it emerged into an actual practical food market with traders shouting at the top of their voices. That guy really wanted me to buy a fish but I still had fond memories of my pasta alla norma.

Ortigia is a little island joined to Syracuse by bridges. There are very few actual roads, everything else is about the width of an alleyway and frequently mostly by pedestrians and the odd nutter on an electric scooter. It's very quaint and the sort of place where you just forget yourself for a while and sigh happily. We zig-zagged down to the southernmost point (via a small lunch stop) but the castle was closed. We did have a little chat with some film students who were making a movie in the castle grounds.

We doubled back, upwards, taking in the SyraMuse museum with its interactive exhibitions about various important people from the history of Syracuse: Archimedes, Saint Lucia, Plato, a chap famous for free-diving... then we wandered through the various museums and exhibits attached to the cathedral. We learned what a monstrance is. Then we visited the actual cathedral, an interesting structure adapted from a Greek temple. You wouldn't know this too much from the inside but when you walk past the outside because you've gotten lost while looking for where you want to eat dinner it's pretty blooming obvious.

We eventually found the restaurant and had a lovely dinner. We were introduced to the owner's cats. One was called 'Itler: on account of his quite prominent cat-moustache, rather than any particular desire to invade Poland or anything like that. By the time we headed back to the airbnb, it was dark. I think when I realised how small the island was, I would not have expected to stay there until after dark. Compared to the rest of Syracuse, you could see it as the main attraction.

## Mount Etna

For our last full day in Syracuse, we took a tour of Mount Etna. We were picked up in Syracuse by Federico in his minibus and we got to know our co-trekkers, two people from LA. Our first stop was a lava tube - these are caused by lava flows from the erupting volcano cooling as they move down the mountain, the outside cools while the interior continues to move. This creates hollows that you can explore nearly two hundred years later while wearing a hard hat on holiday. I don't think I'd ever have decided to go explore a lava tube, but I'm glad I did. 

As we continued up the side of Etna, Federico continued to explain about the vents and various eruptions and the recent activity. As he drove, he pointed out the effects of past eruptions and the kinds of tree that grow up in the years afterwards. At the top, we parked up and put on our jackets and jumpers as it was noticeably cooler now. We went to the loo and then had a coffee (a tea for Ingrid naturally) before heading out on to the mountain. We went for a nice walk up and down the various ridges and hollows. The various mosses and flowers were explained to us. There was a cable car submerged in lava because of a previous flow. The new one had been built next door. The different sizes of ejected rock that get blasted out of the vents during an eruption were explained and shown to us. 

Mists rolled in. The landscape was truly alien. But humans still swarmed everywhere, little groups like ours roved the paths from crater to crater, from ridge to ridge. Plant life spread out across like mould spores in circular blobs. In one direction you could look down the mountain but the view faded into the mist. In the other direction, only mist.

Returning to the minibus and another loo stop, the rain began to come down full pelt. As we descended down to the winery for our next stop - a five-flight wine-tasting and lunch - the rain continued to pour causing floods across the roads that our driver had to navigate very carefully. The wines were very nice though I was grateful that we were given plenty of food to soak it up. We liked the first one best - unusually for us it was a white wine - and we bought a bottle. We waddled back to the minibus and I think all of us (passengers at least) drifted in and out of consciousness on the way back to Syracuse. That evening we napped, rising only to buy some bread at the local store in order to be able to eat something that night.
