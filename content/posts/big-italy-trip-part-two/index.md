In September we went to Italy for ten days. In part one [we flew into Palermo, then took a coach ride across Sicily to Syracuse, before a trip up Mount Etna](/posts/big-italy-trip/). In this post, we take the Intercity Notte train overnight to Milan complete with a ferry ride for both us _and_ the train.

Excitingly, we also travelled with our neighbour and sometime quizmaster Brian. He was our handy translator and selector of fine wines.

Back in January 2017 we went to Italy for a weekend, which I wrote about on the blog as [a little trip to Italy](/posts/a-little-trip-to-italy/). This, by comparison, was a big trip to Italy. So big, in fact, it requires multiple posts; this is the second of three.

## Departure

{{< figure src="images/getting_on.jpg" alt="Quick selfie before getting on board for 18+ hours." caption="Quick selfie before getting on board for 18+ hours." >}}

Our departure from Siracusa was on the Intercity Notte train to Milan. This is an overnight train and the really special feature is how they shunt the entire train onto a ferry in order to cross the Messina Strait. Apparently this is one of only two places left in the world where the train is moved onto the ferry this way.

{{< carousel images="images/sicily/*.jpg" caption="Some snaps from out of the train window as we made our way through Sicily to Messina." />}}

Before we reached Messina the train took a slow sauntering route up the eastern side of Sicily, yielding views of the sea and the beautiful beaches on this part of the island. Not unlike taking the train along the West Sussex coast I suppose, but sunnier and warmer! At our first stop we saw this shunting diesel, which reminded me of 'Rusty' from my childhood train set. At lots of stations they seemed to include old locomotives as sculptures, though this old diesel was on the tracks and was therefore most likely still in use as a shunter.

{{< figure src="images/italian_rusty.jpg" alt="Pilot diesel in the shunting yard at Augusta." caption="Pilot diesel in the shunting yard at Augusta." >}}

After this, I unwrapped the foil and ate my pork and spinach pasty. Delicious. All was good!

## Ferry

Even though the Sicily part of the journey takes a while, it felt like we were on the ferry in next to no time. Here are some pictures of what it looks like inside, with Ingrid and Brian featured throughout for scale.

{{< carousel images="images/loading_*.jpg" caption="Boarding the ferry while on the train." />}}

First order of the day on the ferry was coffee and tea, and for me, a strange hot-dog-looking thing that contained its own tomato ketchup. It was tasty but I won't think too much about the mystery meat contents. However, I will always remember that rather nondescript bit of boat food simply for the pleasure of eating it on the warm sunny deck in the cool sea breeze and all the while I could also look at the train that was on the deck below. 

{{< figure src="images/mystery_meat.jpg" alt="Enjoying the mystery meat roll in the golden hour." caption="Enjoying the mystery meat roll in the golden hour." >}}

{{< figure src="images/train_on_a_boat.jpg" alt="Look! It's a train on a boat. It's hard to convey just how exciting I found this." caption="Look! It's a train on a boat. It's hard to convey just how exciting I found this." >}}

In fact the ferry part seemed to be over too soon. I'd barely finished my coffee before the announcements began and we were heading back down the stairs, back into our cabins. The shunting of the train back onto the mainland was quick and matter-of-fact. So much so, that we seemed to linger at the next station for quite a while. We enjoyed the coolness of the platform and chatted to the guards and fellow passengers.

{{< figure src="images/back_on_board.jpg" alt="A brief stretch of the legs on dry land while the train was waiting at Villa San Giovanni." caption="A brief stretch of the legs on dry land while the train was waiting at Villa San Giovanni." >}}

## The Coast at Sunset

The next part of the journey will also stay in my memory for a long time. As we rattled along the coast, in and out of cliff-side tunnels and past even more resorts and sandy beaches, we raced the setting sun, which glowed orange and luminous beside us. The cool air rushed in through the windows and it was a most beautiful moment. Eventually I attempted to take some photos but none really did it justice and I went back to appreciating the moment.

## Overnight on the train

{{< figure src="images/getting_dark.jpg" alt="In the cabin staring out into the dark. Across the hall you can see the reflection of Brian in his cabin reading on his phone." caption="In the cabin staring out into the dark. Across the hall you can see the reflection of Brian in his cabin reading on his phone." >}}

As darkness fell, a cheery chap appeared and set up our cabins for bed. We brushed our teeth and went for one last trip to the loo. We settled down into our clean comfy bunks. 

I'd wanted to wait until 23.57, our last scheduled stop of the evening, but fatigue overtook us so we turned our lights out. I did what I always do and listened to some music. This cancelled most of the noise but not really the vibration. You could really feel the points as we made our way through the mountains. The sensation was not unlike being on a rollercoaster but sideways on. 

Listening to music usually gives me a good sense of whether I've managed to drop off, but I'll admit that it had been a while since I had listened to my "solid songs" playlist. I'd been listening to it on the plane because it was downloaded to my phone and I figured the same approach might bode well with us going over the mountains. But when I was 'still awake' and found myself coming to the end of the playlist I gave up and switched to earplugs. I must have slept at some point after that because when I decided to get up and get dressed, Ingrid came back from the toilet. Until she opened the door, I had no idea that she was gone!

## Arriving at Milan

So we lay on the beds for a bit, having ordered breakfast for about 8am. We watched the scenery go past as the sun came up and it was completely changed from Sicily. We hadn't seen any sunrise on Sicily but the light was softer here. It reminded me of renaissance landscape paintings.

{{< figure src="images/sunrise_best_attempt.jpg" alt="My best attempt at capturing the sunrise." caption="My best attempt at capturing the sunrise." >}}

After one or two attempts to capture the mood on my phone, I went back to observing rather than photographing. As I did so, the scenery became more industrial and we got to see all those wonderful things on the outskirts of cities (Piacenza I think) that you only ever see from the train. One was a storage depot for cherry-pickers. They were all brand new, all waiting for their long lives of never actually being used to pick cherries. I thought in that moment that that would be the largest number of cherry-pickers I'd ever see in one place my whole life. And then, after a short break, we obviously passed the main storage site for cherry-pickers because there were even more. The same personal best broken twice in five minutes! If only I'd had the camera to hand, you'd be able to see blurry pictures of it.

The cheery chap returned (how does he do it?!) to put our cabins into day mode. Breakfast is about what you would expect from a train with no buffet car about 15 hours into a journey. Coffee was good. The treats were sweet. Ingrid even got a decent gluten free option and some tea. We even 'read' our complimentary copy of _la Repubblica_ and extracted the pages about renewable energy as a souvenir.

We arrived into Milano Centrale slightly early. It was very crowded, partly because almost everyone else's train seemed to be late. It seemed like the entire population of Sicily was in there milling around. We headed out as fast as we could to seek out the hotel, which was mercifully nearby.
